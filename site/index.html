How the list is ordered

  1. Tier first: companies with a physical office in Norway are listed first; those without one are in a separate, lower-priority section below.
  2. In-house security staff is the primary signal. Companies whose count is confirmed sort above those with an estimate, which sort above those where nothing could be established. Within each group, more staff sorts higher. A crowdsourced researcher pool is not a payroll, so those rows sort below every company with staff. Unknown shows ?? rather than a guess.
  3. Employees: the secondary signal, used where staff counts tie or are missing.
  4. A missing number is not a zero. A company with ?? sorts below one with a number because no number has been established, which is a fact about what is known, not about its skill.
  5. The label matters as much as the number. published comes from the company itself; attested from a named person vouching for it first-hand; those two are the confirmed ones. estimated is a guarded estimate by the maintainer, the weakest basis above unknown, and crowd is a researcher pool, not staff.

Fifteen security staff at a specialist boutique and fifteen inside a 4,000-person consultancy are very different propositions, and nothing here is ranked by quality. Read the columns, not the order.

Why there is a delivery column

A lot of security work sold in Norway is not delivered by the company selling it. A services page looks the same whether a firm has forty analysts on its own payroll or one account manager and a subcontractor on speed dial, and the difference matters if you are buying incident response at 03:00 or want to know who is accountable when a test misses something.

So each row says how the work is delivered: in-house when the company's own employees do it, mixed when it is mostly own staff with subcontractors for peaks or specialisms, and partner-sourced when there is no real delivery team here and the work goes to another provider. ? means we have not established it, which says something about our research and nothing about the company. A partner network is a legitimate way to buy: it is just a different thing to buy, and it should not be a surprise after the contract is signed.

! read this before you rely on any number here

  • These numbers are aggregated and some of them are wrong. They come from public sources, from the Norwegian register of legal entities, and from people who know the market. Figures age, companies reorganise, and headcounts shrink as well as grow.
  • AI-assisted tooling was used for the data passes: fetching pages, cross-checking registry records and drafting entries. The original list was plain manual research by the author, and every fact here is reviewed by a human before it lands, but treat it as a starting point for your own checks, not as due diligence.
  • The estimates are the weakest numbers here. They are labelled estimated everywhere they appear, and they can be replaced by a published figure or a named attestation at any time. A registry headcount is never used as a security staff count, because the registry reports whole entities.
  • Ordering is by published numbers, not quality, and nothing here is a recommendation. No company is endorsed, ranked or paid for, and no vendor's work has been tested.

How this list is maintained

Every company has one JSON file in data/companies/. This page, the README and the CSV and JSON exports are all generated from those files, so they cannot disagree with each other.

Norwegian office status, company registration numbers and registry headcounts come from Enhetsregisteret, the Norwegian register of legal entities, which is open data. A headcount stated by the company itself is marked site; a registry figure is marked registry; a staff count somebody vouched for first-hand is marked attested; and one the maintainer estimated from market knowledge is marked estimated. Read the label before the number.

Numbers are never invented. Where a figure is unknown the row says ??, and CI fails the build if a number appears without a source URL behind it. Rows are re-checked annually: CI warns at 12 months and fails at 24. Click any company name for its notes, its capability tags and its sources.

Vendors may correct facts about their own company but not remove competitors, and any company can ask to be removed from the list entirely: the row is deleted from the dataset and every generated file, with a line in the changelog recording that a removal request was honoured. Being listed is not an accusation and leaving is not an admission of anything.
