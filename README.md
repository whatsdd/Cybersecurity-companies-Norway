A crowd-sourced directory of companies that sell cybersecurity and information security work **to
customers in Norway**: penetration testing, red teaming and security assessment, 24/7 SOC and incident
response, cloud and OT security, identity and access, forensics, GRC, privacy and security training.

Two things make it useful rather than just long. First, every row says how many **in-house security
staff** the company has, or that nobody has established it, because a lot of Norwegian security work is
sold by companies that subcontract it to somebody else. Second, every row carries **capability tags**
so a buyer can filter for what they actually need instead of reading 78 company blurbs.

The list comes first and the reasoning follows: the ordering rules, the caveats and the AI disclosure
are further down this page.

## How to read the columns

- **In-house security staff**: the consultants, testers, analysts and engineers on the company's own
  payroll whose job is security. It is the number that answers "can they do this themselves", and it is
  the hardest number to get: almost nobody publishes it, so the marker says what kind of claim it is and
  the marker is worth more than the number:
  - `published`: the company states the figure itself. **Confirmed**.
  - `attested`: a named person vouches for it first-hand, usually because they work there or have run the
    work themselves, and the company does not publish it. **Also confirmed**, and the source line says
    who and why they would know.
  - `estimated`: nobody could confirm it, so the maintainer put a **guarded estimate** in, from having
    worked in this market. It is rounded, it is labelled `estimated` everywhere it appears, and it should
    be treated as a starting point rather than a fact.
  - `crowd`: a crowdsourced platform's pool of contracted researchers, which is not a payroll. Those rows
    sort below every company with staff testers.
  - `??`: no number has been established yet. Click through to send it in.
- **Employees**: the whole company, not the security staff. `site` means the company states it; `registry`
  means it is the headcount filed with Enhetsregisteret for the legal entity. Both carry the date they
  were read, and both are shown when they disagree. A registry figure is never used as a security staff
  count: it covers everyone, including people who never touch security work.
- **Office in Norway**: `yes` requires a registered Norwegian entity or a published Norwegian address.
  `no` means no Norwegian entity was found, which is not the same as "no Norwegian staff".
- **Delivery**: how the Norwegian work is actually done. `in-house` means the company's own employees do
  it, `mixed` means mostly own staff with subcontractors for peaks or specialisms, and `partner-sourced`
  means there is no real delivery team here and the work is resold or subcontracted to somebody else.
  `?` means it has not been established, which is a fact about the research rather than about the company.
  This column exists because a provider and a reseller look identical on a services page and are very
  different things to buy from.
- **Speciality and capabilities**: one plain-language line on what the company is actually known for,
  then the capability tags it publishes. The tags are the filter to use: they include offensive work
  (penetration testing, red teaming, purple teaming, social engineering, attack surface management,
  physical security, crypto audits), defensive work (SOC/MDR, incident response, threat intelligence,
  threat hunting, forensics, malware analysis, endpoint, network, email, IAM, PKI, data protection,
  backup), cloud and industrial work (cloud, OT/ICS, IoT, containers), and governance work (audit,
  compliance, risk, privacy/GDPR, NIS2/DORA readiness, virtual CISO, training, tabletop exercises).
- **Org. no.**: the nine-digit Norwegian organisasjonsnummer for the entity that delivers the Norwegian
  work, the reliable way to tell providers apart after renames and acquisitions.
- **Last updated**: when a human last checked the row. Green within 6 months, amber to 12, red beyond.

## How this list is ordered

1. **Tier first.** Companies with a physical office in Norway are listed first because that is what most
   Norwegian buyers need. Companies without one are in a separate, lower-priority section.
2. **In-house security staff, the primary signal.** A company that can staff the work itself sorts above
   one whose count is only an estimate, which sorts above one where no count has been established. More
   staff sorts higher within those groups. A crowdsourced researcher pool is not a payroll, so those rows
   sort below every company with staff. Where the number is not known the row shows `??` rather than a
   guess, and there is an [issue form](https://github.com/whatsdd/Cybersecurity-companies-Norway/issues/new?template=update-company.yml)
   to send it in.
3. **Employees, the secondary signal.** Where staff counts tie or are equal, total headcount sorts next.
4. **A missing number is not a zero.** A company with `??` sorts below one with a number purely because no
   number has been established, which is a fact about what is known, not about its skill.
5. **The label matters as much as the number.** See [How to read the columns](#how-to-read-the-columns)
   above. An estimate sorts like a number but reads as an estimate.

Fifteen security staff at a specialist boutique and fifteen inside a 4,000-person consultancy are very
different propositions, and neither is ranked by quality here. Use the columns, not the order.

## Read this before you rely on any number here

- **These are aggregated numbers, and some of them are wrong.** They are collected from public sources,
  from the Norwegian register of legal entities, and from people who know the market. Figures age,
  companies reorganise, and headcounts shrink as well as grow.
- **AI-assisted tooling was used for the data passes**: fetching pages, cross-checking registry records and
  drafting entries. The original list was plain manual research by the author, and the facts in this
  repository are reviewed by a human before they land, but treat every figure here as a starting point for
  your own check rather than as due diligence.
- **The estimates are the weakest numbers in the dataset.** They carry an `estimated` label wherever they
  appear, they are documented in [docs/methodology.md](docs/methodology.md#guarded-estimates), and they can
  be replaced by a published figure or a named attestation at any time.
- **The delivery column is a claim like any other.** Where it says `partner-sourced` it is because the
  company or a named person said so, or because the work is visibly delivered by another provider. Absence
  of a delivery label means we have not established it.
- **Ordering is by published and estimated numbers, not by quality.** See
  [How this list is ordered](#how-this-list-is-ordered).
- **Nothing here is a recommendation.** No company is endorsed, ranked, certified or paid for by this list,
  and nobody has tested the vendors' work.

## Corrections, additions and removals

- **Corrections with a source are the most valuable contribution there is.** Open an issue or a pull
  request; a URL beats a memory every time.
- **Vendors may correct their own row** and may not delete a competitor's.
- **Any company can ask to be removed from this list**, no reason required, and the removal is honoured.
  Open an issue with the [removal form](https://github.com/whatsdd/Cybersecurity-companies-Norway/issues/new?template=remove-company.yml)
  and the row is deleted from the dataset and from every generated file on merge, with a line in
  [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md) recording that a removal happened without dwelling on who asked.
  Being listed is not an accusation and leaving is not an admission of anything.
- **A company can also ask for one figure to be dropped** while staying listed, for example if it does not
  want its staff count published. Say which figure and it is removed; the rest of the row stays.

## How this list is maintained

- **Data lives in [`data/companies/`](data/companies)**, one JSON file per company. That file is the single
  source of truth; this README, the [website](https://whatsdd.github.io/Cybersecurity-companies-Norway/) and the CSV/JSON/YAML exports are all generated
  from it, so they cannot disagree.
- **Every figure carries its source and its basis**, and CI fails the build if a number appears without
  one. `scripts/validate.py` is the enforcement.
- **Registry headcounts can never be passed off as security staff counts.** The validator refuses it,
  because Enhetsregisteret reports the whole entity and not what it does.
- **A delivery model cannot be asserted without a source**, and `partner-sourced` or `mixed` has to say how
  that was established.
- **Rows are re-checked annually.** CI warns when a row is over 12 months old and fails at 24.
- **Third-party flags are welcome, machinery is not.** Tools that scrape the data are fine; contributions
  are reviewed by a human.

```bash
python3 scripts/validate.py     # check the dataset
python3 scripts/build.py        # regenerate README, site and exports
```
